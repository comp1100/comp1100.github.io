Week 8: Competitor analysis and Iteration 2 project work#

Studio abstract#

In this studio, your team will define how it intends to get, keep and grow customers, connect those choices to its channels, identify direct and indirect competitors and current alternatives, and begin the competitor comparison required for Iteration 2. You will use the remaining time to address the team’s highest-priority Iteration 2 gap.

The activities total 140 minutes and begin after the 30-minute innovation seminar and a 10-minute transition.

By the end of the studio, your team should have:

  • updated the Customer Relationships and Channels sections of its Business Model Canvas;

  • recorded important assumptions behind its customer-relationship choices;

  • identified direct competitors, indirect competitors and current alternatives;

  • started the assessed comparison of 4–6 competitors plus its own product;

  • for COMP7110, allocated one competitor Business Model Canvas to each team member; and

  • advanced its highest-priority remaining Iteration 2 task.

From the seminar to your project#

The seminar connected customer relationships with channels and introduced competitor analysis as a way to test whether a value proposition is genuinely distinctive. Apply those ideas to your team’s current evidence. Do not assume that having a different feature, or not knowing of a similar product, means that there are no competitors. Customers already have ways of addressing, or avoiding, the problem.

The competitor-analysis activities draw on Introduction to Software Innovation: Value Proposition Canvas, Competitor Analyses (pp. 54–57 in the PDF edition).

1. Define how you will get, keep and grow customers#

⏱️ 30 minutes (team)

Open the team’s current Business Model Canvas and customer evidence. For one priority customer segment, discuss and record how the team intends to:

Relationship purpose

Team decision

Get

How will customers become aware, interested and willing to try the proposed solution?

Keep

What product experience, support, communication or engagement will encourage them to continue?

Grow

How might referrals, increased use, cross-selling or upselling deepen the relationship?

For each decision, identify the channel through which the relationship occurs. Check that the channel suits both the product and how the customer already discovers, evaluates, obtains and uses solutions.

Choose the most important relationship assumption that is not yet supported by evidence. Record it as a hypothesis or uncertainty to test; do not present it as an established fact.

Update the Customer Relationships and Channels sections of the Business Model Canvas. The result can be concise, but another person should be able to understand:

  • the intended relationship with the priority customer segment;

  • where and how the interaction occurs;

  • the evidence supporting the choice; and

  • what remains uncertain.

2. Identify competitors and current alternatives#

⏱️ 25 minutes (team)

Begin by writing a short statement for the comparison:

Our project, [project name], aims to solve [brief problem statement] for [target customer segment]. We believe our unique value proposition is [value proposition].

Treat the final sentence as a claim to investigate, not a conclusion. Begin with the customer problem rather than a list of products that resemble your proposed solution. Generate candidates in three groups:

Group

What to look for

Direct competitors

A similar type of solution for the same customer segment and problem

Indirect competitors

A different type of solution for the same customer segment and problem

Current alternatives

Workarounds, services, behaviours or doing nothing

Use more than one way to find candidates:

  1. Customer interviews: revisit customer interviews and note what people use now;

  2. Search engines: search online using language a customer would use;

  3. Customer channels: inspect the channels through which customers find or obtain solutions;

  4. Social media: review social-media recommendations and complaints; and

  5. Industry sources: consult specialist forums, blogs, reports and events.

Record the source for useful claims as you work. Do not rely only on competitor marketing material, and do not invent details where evidence is unavailable.

Select 4–6 competitors for the assessed comparison. Aim for a useful mix of close competitors and meaningful alternatives rather than six nearly identical products.

3. Start the Iteration 2 competitor comparison#

⏱️ 35 minutes (team)

Create the competitor table in a clear location in the repository. Include the team’s proposed product as a comparison column or row in addition to the 4–6 competitors.

Use the assessed fields:

Field

Question to answer

Competitor company or product

What is being compared?

Target market

Which customers or users does it appear to serve?

Key value propositions

What value does it promise or provide?

Weaknesses

What limitations, gaps or trade-offs are supported by evidence?

Notes

What other evidence or context affects the comparison?

Add links or citations for the evidence supporting each entry. Keep entries concise and specific. A weakness should be a defensible limitation or trade-off, not an unsupported negative judgement.

After completing at least two competitor entries and your own-product entry, discuss:

  1. Which claims about your value proposition still appear distinctive?

  2. Which claims are common across the market?

  3. What unmet need, weakness or trade-off deserves further investigation?

  4. What new question or hypothesis should the team test?

Record these as implications or questions, not certainty. Link any resulting hypothesis or project decision back to the comparison evidence.

Additional COMP7110 requirement#

Each COMP7110 team member must research the business model of one competitor from the team’s comparison and document it as a Business Model Canvas.

  • Allocate one competitor to each team member during this activity.

  • Use different competitors where team size permits.

  • The allocated competitors count among the team’s 4–6 competitors; they are not additional competitors.

  • Add an owned task for each canvas to the team’s GitHub Project.

  • Agree where the canvases will be stored in the repository.

Break#

⏱️ 10 minutes

4. Address the highest-priority Iteration 2 gap#

⏱️ 30 minutes (team project work and optional legend review)

Review the current Iteration 2 submission checklist. Identify the most important incomplete or weak requirement, considering:

  • solution prototype, testing evidence and linked learning;

  • updated Business Model and Value Proposition Canvases;

  • hypotheses, tests, evidence and learning cards;

  • interview transcripts and recording links;

  • competitor analysis; and

  • repository clarity and traceability.

Select one task that can be completed or substantially advanced during this period. Give it one accountable owner, a clear outcome and a deadline. Other team members may support the task or work on the next-highest priorities, but avoid starting several new pieces of work without finishing any.

If time permits, ask your legend to inspect either the competitor comparison or the selected Iteration 2 gap. Be ready to show the relevant repository files and explain what evidence supports the work.

Exit ticket and next steps#

⏱️ 10 minutes (team and legend)

Show your legend:

  1. the updated Customer Relationships and Channels entries in the Business Model Canvas;

  2. one important customer-relationship assumption that still requires testing;

  3. the selected direct competitors, indirect competitors and current alternatives;

  4. the started competitor table, including evidence sources and the team’s own product;

  5. for COMP7110, the per-member competitor-canvas allocations and repository location; and

  6. the highest-priority Iteration 2 task advanced during the studio and what remains to be done.

After the studio, complete the competitor comparison and test any important assumptions it reveals. Keep the comparison traceable to its sources, update hypotheses and canvases when the evidence changes the team’s understanding, and finish the remaining Iteration 2 work before creating the submission tag.
